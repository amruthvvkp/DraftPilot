# Story tools

## Story twin

The Story twin is the room's model of your story. DraftPilot derives it from your working draft and
keeps it up to date:

- **Characters**, from dialogue cues: how many lines each speaks, in how many scenes, and where
  each first appears. They appear on the **Characters** page.
- **Locations**, from scene headings: interior or exterior, the times of day used, and the scenes
  each appears in. They appear on the **Locations** page.

It refreshes a few seconds after you stop editing. You can also click **Refresh from the draft**.
Edit any character or location yourself, and the twin keeps your edit: derived facts update around
your note, but your words are never overwritten. Every agent reads a short brief of the twin before
it works, so the room always knows who is in your story.

## Writer twin

The Writer twin holds who you are and how you work. It is made up of:

- your profile, style notes, preferences and things to avoid (**Settings → Writer profile**);
- memories you pin, whether for every project or for just one;
- what the room has learned from your decisions, meaning how often you approve or reject its
  proposals.

The room gives the Writer twin priority over any specialist's opinion.

## Knowledge graph

The knowledge graph (**Creative context → Knowledge graph**) holds canon the typed pages don't:
themes, rules of the world, objects and any other node you add, with the connections you draw
between them. Edits are versioned. Characters and locations live on their own pages
(see [Develop a story](guided-flow.md)), and the Story twin writes there, not into the graph.

## Story studio

The story studio holds your free-form development artifacts, such as research notes, canon and
reference documents. When an artifact another one depends on changes, the dependent artifact is
marked stale.

The story itself (storyline, characters, locations, timelines and the outline) is typed data on its
own pages. Room workflows propose changes there: for example, **Notes → outline** proposes a logline
for the storyline and beats for the outline, and **Outline → scenes** drafts from the outline's
beats. To arrange beats across acts, use the **Outline** page.

## Creative context

Context workflows generate cited, review-only suggestions: reference scenes, visual language, camera,
lighting, palettes and continuity notes. See [Context workflows](context-workflows.md).
